The `refract` tier models a real piece of glass resting on the page: flat on the bottom, flat on
top, with a curved rim, the **bezel**, where the two meet. Light passing through the flat middle
goes straight down. Light hitting the bezel bends, so what you see near the edge comes from
further inside, squeezed toward the rim.

Drag the panel over the stripes and text, and change the material:

## The model

For a point at distance `s` across the bezel (0 at the rim, 1 where the glass turns flat):

1. **Surface height** follows a convex squircle, which gives the steep, liquid-looking edge:
   `h(s) = thickness · (1 − (1 − s)⁴)^¼`.
2. **Incidence:** a vertical view ray meets the surface at angle `θ₁ = atan(h′(s))` from its normal.
3. **Snell's law:** inside the glass it travels at `θ₂ = asin(sin θ₁ / n)`, where `n` is the
   index of refraction.
4. **Displacement:** the ray now leans by `θ₁ − θ₂` and crosses the glass's height before reaching
   the page, so it lands `h(s) · tan(θ₁ − θ₂)` px further in, toward the center.

The result is zero at the very rim (no glass height) and zero in the flat middle (no bend), with a
sharp peak just inside the edge. With the bezel as tall as it is wide, the peak is about
`0.42 × thickness`.

In the corners the same profile runs along the distance to the rounded rectangle, bending
radially.

## From optics to pixels

`displacementMap()` evaluates the model for every pixel and stores the result in an image: red for
horizontal shift, green for vertical, with 128 meaning none. The strongest shift is stretched to the
full 8-bit range, so precision stays below a third of a pixel for normal sizes. The map is drawn
at up to 2× the CSS size on high-density screens.

The browser does the rest on the GPU:

```
backdrop ──► feDisplacementMap (map, scale) ──► + rim highlight ──► blur ──► saturate
```

The blue channel carries the **rim highlight**: a crisp line plus a soft glow, brightest where the
rim faces a light at the top left.

## Options

| Option      | Default                   | Effect                                                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `thickness` | `24`                      | Height of the glass in px. Thicker glass bends more. `0` turns bending off.          |
| `ior`       | `1.5`                     | Index of refraction: water `1.33`, glass `1.5`, sapphire `1.77`, diamond `2.42`.     |
| `bezel`     | `20`                      | Width of the curved rim in px. Narrower rims look harder and more liquid.            |
| `radius`    | `18`                      | Corner radius in px. Match the element's `border-radius`, or the corners bend wrong. |
| `specular`  | `0.6`                     | Rim highlight strength, 0 to 1.                                                      |
| `blur`      | `var(--gm-blur-md, 16px)` | Frost applied after bending. Lower values show more of the refraction.               |

```ts
const glass = createGlass(el, { radius: 28, thickness: 32, ior: 1.5 });
glass.update({ ior: 1.33 }); // turn it into water
```

## Cost

The map is computed on the CPU when the surface is created and whenever its size or optics change.
**Moving it costs nothing extra**: the map depends only on size, so dragging or scrolling glass
just moves the filter with the element. Avoid animating its size or optics every frame.
