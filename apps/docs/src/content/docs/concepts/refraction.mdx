The `refract` tier models a real piece of glass resting on the page: flat on the bottom, flat on
top, with a curved rim, the **bezel**, where the two meet. Light passing through the flat middle
goes straight down. Light hitting the bezel bends, so what you see near the edge comes from
further inside, squeezed toward the rim.

## The model

The glass is `thickness` px tall. Its curved rim rises over the bezel width; whatever thickness is
left over is a flat slab underneath. For a point at distance `s` across the bezel (0 at the rim, 1
where the glass turns flat):

1. **Surface height** follows a convex squircle, which joins the flat top without a visible ring:
   `h(s) = rise · (1 − (1 − s)⁴)^¼`, with `rise = min(thickness, bezel)`.
2. **Incidence:** a vertical view ray meets the surface at angle `θ₁ = atan(h′(s))` from its normal.
3. **Snell's law:** inside the glass it travels at `θ₂ = asin(sin θ₁ / n)`, where `n` is the
   index of refraction.
4. **Displacement:** the ray now leans by `θ₁ − θ₂` and crosses the rest of the glass,
   `slab + h(s)`, before reaching the page, so it lands `(slab + h(s)) · tan(θ₁ − θ₂)` px further
   in, toward the center.

Bending is strongest near the rim and fades to zero where the glass turns flat. In the corners the
same profile runs along the distance to the rounded rectangle, bending radially.

### The whole surface is a lens

With only a curved rim, the flat middle of the glass shows the backdrop unchanged, and the material
reads as glass only at its edges. Two options carry it across the whole component:

- **`magnify`** (default `0`) samples the whole surface toward its center, so everything behind it
  looks `1 / (1 − magnify)` times larger, like through a magnifier.
- **A bezel as wide as half the smaller side** curves the entire surface. With **`curvature: 2`**
  the profile is a circle: a pillow of glass rather than a slab with rounded edges.

Pick **Thick lens** in the demo above to see both together.

### Caustics: the side wall

Near the rim of real glass, light bends so hard that the image folds over itself into a
compressed, mirrored band of the backdrop. That band reads as the **side wall of the glass** and
gives it depth. glass-midas renders it by default (`caustics: true`): the default 40 px of glass
over a 20 px bezel folds a thin, crisp band (the **Crisp rim** preset), and
`{ thickness: 160, bezel: 60, curvature: 2 }` gives the wide band of a thick lens.

Keep blur low: frost is applied before the bend and smears the band away.

### Without caustics

`caustics: false` caps local magnification at 5× instead, so the image never folds. Where the
physical curve is within that limit it's used as is; the caustic band becomes a smooth, strongly
compressed edge. Magnification counts toward the limit, so the rim gets less room the more the
surface magnifies. It's the **Subtle** look in the demo.

## From optics to pixels

`displacementMap()` evaluates the model across the bezel once, then looks it up for every pixel and
stores the result in an image: red for horizontal shift, green for vertical, with 128 meaning
none. The strongest shift is stretched to the full 8-bit range: under a tenth of a pixel per step
for thin glass, but over a pixel for thick glass with caustics, which can show as faint banding.
The map is drawn at up to 2× the CSS size on high-density screens.

The browser does the rest on the GPU:

```
backdrop ──► blur ──► feDisplacementMap (map) ──► + saturated rim ──► + white rim
```

Frost comes first so the rim light added at the end stays sharp. Without caustics, a 0.6 px
smoothing after the bend hides the blocky look of magnified pixels.

### Dispersion

Real glass bends red a little less than blue, so a strong edge splits into a thin rainbow.
`chroma` does the same: the backdrop is bent three times, red at `1 − chroma` times the bend
and blue at `1 + chroma`, and each pass keeps one color channel. Where the glass is flat the
three agree, so only the rim splits. Real glass is about `0.01`; `0.05` to `0.15` shows. It
triples the cost of the bend, so it's off by default: keep it for a few hero surfaces.

### Rim light

The edge that makes the glass read as a solid object is a **hairline**, about 1.5 px wide and
centered 1.25 px inside the rim, carried in the map's blue channel. Its brightness is `(n·L)²`, the
squared alignment between the rim's direction and the light (`lightAngle`, default −60°), so the
sides facing toward and away from the light glow and the ones across it fade. It's drawn twice:

1. A copy of the bent backdrop with its saturation multiplied by `specularSaturation` (default
   `4`), shown only through the hairline. The rim glows with the colors behind the glass.
2. The hairline in white at `specular` opacity (default `0.2`).

## Options

| Option               | Default | Effect                                                                               |
| -------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `thickness`          | `40`    | Total height of the glass in px. Thicker bends more. `0` turns bending off.          |
| `ior`                | `1.5`   | Index of refraction: water `1.33`, glass `1.5`, sapphire `1.77`, diamond `2.42`.     |
| `bezel`              | `20`    | Width of the curved rim in px. Narrower rims look harder and more liquid.            |
| `radius`             | `18`    | Corner radius in px. Match the element's `border-radius`, or the corners bend wrong. |
| `curvature`          | `4`     | Rim profile: `2` circle, `4` squircle.                                               |
| `magnify`            | `0`     | Magnifies the whole surface, `0` to `0.5`.                                           |
| `caustics`           | `true`  | Fold the rim into a mirrored band. `false` caps magnification at 5×.                 |
| `specular`           | `0.2`   | Opacity of the white rim hairline.                                                   |
| `specularSaturation` | `4`     | Saturation of the backdrop seen through the hairline.                                |
| `lightAngle`         | `-60`   | Where the rim light comes from, in degrees: `0` right, `-90` top.                    |
| `chroma`             | `0`     | Dispersion, 0 to 0.5: the rim splits into color. Costs three bends instead of one.   |
| `blur`               | `1px`   | Frost applied before bending. Keep it low to see the fold.                           |

```ts
const glass = createGlass(el, { radius: 28, thickness: 32, ior: 1.5 });
glass.update({ ior: 1.33 }); // turn it into water
```

## Cost

The map is computed on the CPU when the surface is created and whenever its size or optics change.
**Moving it costs nothing extra**: the map depends only on size, so dragging or scrolling glass
just moves the filter with the element. Avoid animating its size or optics every frame.
